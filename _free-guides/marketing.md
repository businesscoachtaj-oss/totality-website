This guide is for trainers who can coach but don't yet have a steady flow of new enquiries, whether you're just starting or have relied on word of mouth so far.

No single channel fills a diary. The chapters follow the order that works: get your message clear, set up the places you own, then add social media, referrals and ads once the basics work. Jumping straight to ads is the most common way trainers waste money.

:::box A note on accuracy
Platforms change, and two Australian laws in here carry real obligations: the Spam Act 2003 and Australian Consumer Law. Check current rules with ACMA, the ACCC or your adviser before you rely on them.
:::

# Know who you're talking to

Most marketing that fails was too general to make anyone stop. Before you spend time or money, get two things clear.

## A niche you can say out loud

"I help people get fit" describes almost every trainer in the country. "I help busy parents fit strength training around school pick ups" or "I help runners come back from injury" tells a particular person you mean them. You can still train whoever walks in. The niche is for your message.

## One line that says what you do

Fill in: **I help [who] get [result] through [how you coach], without [what they're worried about].** If you can't fill in all four clearly, that's usually why your marketing isn't landing.

## Turn your training into an offer

"Personal training sessions" is hard to say yes to, because it doesn't say what happens. An offer has:

- **A result**, stated plainly.
- **A timeframe**, like 6 or 12 weeks, or a clear monthly cycle.
- **A format**: one on one, small group, or online.
- **What's included**: sessions a week, programme, check-ins, nutrition guidance, progress tracking.
- **One price** for the whole thing.

Give it a name you can use everywhere, like "12 Week Strength Reset". Then build a simple ladder: an easy first step (a trial week or an assessment), your main offer, and an ongoing option for when it ends (monthly coaching or a plan).

:::box Examples
For busy parents: "6 Week Strength Foundations", two 30 minute sessions a week in school hours. For runners: "Return to Running", 8 weeks one on one, with a physio check in week one. Same trainer, two clear offers.
:::

# Set up the places you own

Social media can change its rules or reach overnight. A website, a Google listing and your own contact list are yours.

## Your Google Business Profile

When someone searches "personal trainer near me", Google's local results come first. A complete free Google Business Profile, with your hours, service area, real photos and genuine reviews, is one of the cheapest things you can do for local clients.

## A simple website

One page is enough: what you do, who it's for, a couple of real testimonials, and how to book or get in touch. It saves people digging through your bio for a phone number.

## Your own list

Keep every enquiry, trial client and past client on an email or text list, with their permission to contact them. It survives an algorithm change or a hacked account.

:::box The Spam Act 2003
To send marketing emails or texts you need consent (an enquiry or an existing client counts, a bought or scraped list doesn't). Every message must say who it's from and have an unsubscribe that works and that you honour promptly.
:::

# Social media that's worth your time

Posts build trust when they're specific and regular. Most followers won't see most posts, so repeating your main message is a good thing.

## Pick three or four themes and stick to them

| Theme | What to post |
|---|---|
| Teaching | A 30 second form fix, why you chose an exercise, a real question a client asked |
| Behind the scenes | Your own training, how you build a client's week, why you coach |
| Results | A client's story as their result, in their words, with permission |
| Your business | New spots, a programme opening, a milestone. Keep this the smallest share |

## One main platform

Pick the one your niche already uses and get good at it. Repost to others if you like.

## A pace you can keep

Two or three posts a week, every week, beats seven one week and nothing for three. Plan a week or two in one sitting.

## Match the idea to the format

Short videos reach people who don't follow you yet. Carousels and useful posts get saved by people who do. Stories suit small everyday moments. Clear beats polished.

:::box Keep a list
Every question a client or follower asks you is a post. Write them down as they come.
:::

# Referrals and local partners

Referrals are the cheapest, most trusted source of clients, and the one most left to chance.

- **Ask when it's earned.** Right after a client hits a milestone or someone notices their progress. Build it into a regular point, like every 6 to 8 weeks.
- **Make it easy.** A link, a referral code, or "bring a friend to a free session" gives them something to act on.
- **Partner locally.** Physios, chiros, sports stores and cafes near you see your ideal clients and don't compete with you. Send people their way and ask them to do the same.

People refer friends when they're confident their friend will be looked after. Good coaching is the base every referral system rests on.

# Paid ads, when you're ready

Ads make what already works reach more people. Run them only once your offer, replies and booking work.

- **Start small** and track **cost per paying client**, not clicks or likes.
- **Facebook and Instagram** are usually the easiest place to start for a local trainer: target your area and interests. Google search ads can work for "personal trainer [suburb]", but cost more per click in big cities.
- **Send people to a simple page** with one next step, not your Instagram feed.
- **Give it two to three weeks** before you judge it.

A clear offer matters more than a clever ad. If your one line from chapter 1 isn't done, do that first.

# Turning enquiries into clients

What happens in the hours after someone messages you decides whether they book.

- **Reply the same day**, ideally within a few hours. People often book with whoever answers first and clearly.
- **Give one next step**, like a free intro session or a 15 minute call. Not five options and a price list.
- **Ask before you sell.** Goals, history and what gets in their way. It shows you care, and it tells you whether you're a good fit.
- **Let them book directly.** A booking link beats a back and forth about times.
- **Keep every enquiry in one place you check.** DMs, missed calls, forms and walk-ins all need to end up somewhere you see.

# Keeping clients

Keeping a client costs less than finding a new one, and happy clients bring referrals.

- **Collect testimonials regularly**, when clients are pleased with their progress, with permission to use them.
- **Check in with past clients** now and then with a genuine message, not a mass blast. They already know how you coach.
- **Talk about what's next before a package ends**, while they're still training.

:::box Keep result claims honest
Under Australian Consumer Law, testimonials must be genuine and any claim you make must be one you can back up. The ACCC has acted against fitness and weight loss promoters over guaranteed results. Show a client's result as theirs, not as a promise.
:::

# Your marketing checklist

- [ ] Write your one line: who, result, how, without what
- [ ] Turn your training into a named offer with one price
- [ ] Claim and complete your Google Business Profile
- [ ] Make a simple one page website
- [ ] Start a contact list with consent from day one
- [ ] Check your emails and texts meet the Spam Act
- [ ] Choose 3 or 4 post themes and one main platform
- [ ] Set a posting pace you can keep
- [ ] Ask for referrals at a regular point
- [ ] Give clients an easy way to refer
- [ ] Find 2 or 3 local businesses to refer with
- [ ] Hold off on ads until your offer and replies work
- [ ] Track cost per paying client on any ad
- [ ] Reply to every enquiry the same day
- [ ] Give every enquiry one clear next step
- [ ] Collect testimonials with permission
- [ ] Keep result claims honest
- [ ] Check in with past clients regularly
