Fibre: how much you need and the foods that have it

Keeps your digestion regular, feeds the good bacteria in your gut and helps you feel full for longer. Adult men need about 30 g a day and women 25 g.

Most Australians eat less than they need. Eating enough is linked to a lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and bowel cancer.

Questions people ask

How much fibre do I need a day?

Adult men need about 30 g a day and women 25 g. These are the NHMRC Nutrient Reference Values for Australia and New Zealand.

Which foods are highest in fibre?

Per 100 g, the foods with the most are wattle seed (ground), hemp flour, linseed or flaxseed seed, wheat bran breakfast cereal (pellets), garlic (peeled, fresh). The table on this page lists the top 20.

What does fibre do?

Keeps your digestion regular, feeds the good bacteria in your gut and helps you feel full for longer.

Where do these numbers come from?

Food figures are from the Australian Food Composition Database from Food Standards Australia New Zealand. Daily amounts are from the Australian food labelling rules and the NHMRC Nutrient Reference Values.

Where these numbers come from. Nutrition figures are from the Australian Food Composition Database (AFCD), Release 3, © Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), used under the AFCD Data User Licence. The figures on this page are shared under the same licence. We rounded the values, converted energy to calories as well as kilojoules, grouped cooking methods for the same food together and worked out amounts for other weights. This site is not endorsed by or connected with FSANZ.

There are limitations associated with food composition databases. Food composition data used in the database or databases may represent an average of the nutrient content of a particular sample of foods and ingredients, determined at a particular time. The nutrient composition of foods and ingredients can vary substantially between batches and brands because of a number of factors, including changes in season, processing practices and ingredient source, and methods of calculation.

The AFCD is based on Australian data and may not be appropriate for use in other countries. General information only, not medical or dietary advice.
