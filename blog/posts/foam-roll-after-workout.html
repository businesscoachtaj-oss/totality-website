Foam Roll After Your Workout, Not Just Before

September 19, 2026  ·  Totality

Most people only reach for a foam roller as part of their warm-up, then pack it away the second the session starts. The research says that's backwards.

Spend an extra 10-20 seconds rolling each major muscle group you just trained, right after your session, not only before it. Post-workout rolling appears to meaningfully ease next-day soreness, which matters for keeping clients showing up to their next session instead of skipping it while sore. Pre-workout rolling, by contrast, mainly helps with sprint speed and flexibility, not strength or recovery.

What the Research Found

A 2019 meta-analysis pooling 21 studies found post-exercise foam rolling cut delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) by a moderate amount, an effect size of g=0.47. That was the largest benefit the researchers measured for any rolling protocol, whether applied before or after a workout.

How to Use It

Keep the pre-workout roll if it's part of your warm-up routine; it still has a place for mobility and sprint prep. But don't stop there. Add a short post-session pass over whatever you just trained: quads and hamstrings after leg day, lats and pecs after upper body. It costs two minutes and the evidence says it pays off the next morning.

Evidence

  • Wiewelhove T, et al. (2019), Frontiers in Physiology, 10:376. A meta-analysis of 21 studies on foam rolling for performance and recovery. PMID: 31024339

This is general information, not individual medical advice. If soreness is severe or persistent, check with a physiotherapist or doctor before continuing to train through it.
