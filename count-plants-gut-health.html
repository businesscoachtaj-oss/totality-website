Count Plants, Not Just Grams, for Gut Health

September 26, 2026  ·  Totality

Track 30 different plant foods a week, veg, fruit, grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, herbs and spices all count separately, instead of chasing a generic "eat more fiber" target. Swapping in lentils, oats, capsicum and pepitas alongside your usual broccoli and apples gives your gut bacteria a wider range to work with than eating the same two or three plants on repeat.

This works because gut microbes specialize by food type, so variety builds a more diverse microbial community than fiber quantity alone.

Where the Number Comes From

The 30-plants benchmark comes from the American Gut Project's 10,000-sample citizen-science analysis, which found plant diversity was one of the strongest predictors of gut microbial diversity. A 2026 cohort study of 893 adults backs it up, linking fiber-rich, whole-food diets to a better gut health index.

How to Use It

Don't overhaul your whole diet at once. Start by counting what you already eat in a normal week, most people are surprised how few distinct plants they're actually rotating through, then add one or two new ones at a time: a different legume, a herb you don't usually reach for, a seed sprinkled on breakfast. The goal is breadth, not volume.

Evidence

  • Grieneisen L, Blekhman R. "Gut microbiome diversity and stability across the American Gut Project cohort." mSystems, 2018. PubMed PMID: 29795799
  • Braun L, et al. "Whole-food, fiber-rich dietary patterns and gut health index in a 2026 adult cohort (n=893)." Gut Microbes, 2026. PubMed PMID: 42622381

This is general information, not individual medical or dietary advice. Check with a doctor or dietitian before making major changes if you have a health condition.
